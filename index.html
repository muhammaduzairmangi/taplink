What NFC actually does

NFC lets a customer interact with your business by holding their phone near a card, stand or tag — no app, no typing.

Tap

The customer holds their phone near the product for a second.

→

Open

Their phone opens a prompt automatically — nothing to install.

→

Connect

They land exactly where you sent them — a review page, a menu, a chat.

Depending on the product, a single tap can open Google Reviews, WhatsApp, Instagram, your website, a digital menu, a contact card, a portfolio, or any custom URL you choose.

Frequently asked questions

NFC (Near Field Communication) is a short-range wireless technology already built into most modern smartphones. It lets two devices exchange a small amount of information when held close together — in our case, that information is a link to wherever you want to send your customer.

Each card contains a small chip programmed with a destination, such as your Google review page or WhatsApp number. When a customer holds their phone near the card, their phone reads that chip and opens the destination automatically.

No. NFC works through the phone's built-in reader. The customer doesn't need to install anything — they simply tap and a prompt appears.

Almost all smartphones sold in the last several years support NFC, including current iPhones and the large majority of Android phones. Every product we make also includes a QR code as a backup.

A small number of Android phones have NFC turned off by default. If a tap doesn't work, the customer can simply scan the QR code printed on the same product instead.

QR codes work on virtually any smartphone camera, with no dependence on NFC settings. Including both means every customer can connect, regardless of their phone.
